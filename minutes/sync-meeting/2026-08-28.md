### "Suggest consulting mods when discussing candidates for LC (s)elections" leadership-council#327

- **Link:** https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/issues/327
- **Labels:** T-leadership-council, I-council-nominated

TC: Rémy, please tell us about this.

Rémy: Since we're in election/selection season, I was looking at some of the feedback in the council survey. Wanted to discuss the idea of consulting mods as an explicit part of the guidelines when selecting council members. The process is team-based, so it's possible to miss information. Want to make sure that teams are aware if there are possible issues. Checking with mods privately just to have information on whether the candidates are in a good standing with respect to moderation. We do this across the Project in ad hoc ways. For compiler we do this for team membership. The funding team does this when selecting MiRs (asking team leads and mods, etc.). We should at least discuss the possibility. I don't mean that mods should have blocking concerns; it's more like suggestions.

Manish: I agree in general. Want to point out a possible wrinkle: a fair amount of times, the mods will have a partial profile based on that person having reports that haven't yet been escalated to that person due to being mild or that they haven't put together a pattern of behavior. There are times where mods are being conservative about contacting a person. This puts them in a position where to reveal to a team that a person has complaints against them might require that they reveal to the person that there are complaints against them. They could tell the facilitator that, but that then puts the facilitator in a difficult position. Or it could be treated as a forcing function for talking with the person. This is a potential problem that could come up, but I don't think it's that big of a deal. If the mods are considering real evidence, then they should be open with talking with that person about it.

Josh: Valid point, but we already face this throughout the Project. It's also good for moderation to know, when there are low-to-mid-level reports, about the intent to maybe elevate that person to a more prominent role, as that may prompt them to decide whether they want to act on it. It provides more information; they have the discretion to make use of that information. Enthusiastic support for encouraging conversation between moderation and facilitators.

Frank: Agree with Manish's analysis, but at the same time it's probably more of a problem if we don't get involved. The role implies a demand for "even better" behavior than the average. We want to pay attention to the minor things when a person is going for a leadership position. I agree with the procedure of just talking to a mod without a rigid procedure or strict requirement.

Lori: This has been very helpful to the Funding team. Jakub's been helpful in veriying who we're talking to before we talked to them. You can only know so many people in the Project, so it's good to have this view. I think it's a great use of mods' time.

TC: Any other thoughts?

Rémy: Do you think we should FCP this?

TC: My proposal would be to create a PR against the guidelines and then FCP the language for it.

Rémy: Will do.

TC: Great. Thank you. Next up, let's talk about the PR to document our current observer policies.

### "Allow observers to participate during meetings" leadership-council#110

- **Link:** https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/pull/110
- **Labels:** T-leadership-council, I-council-nominated

TC: This is a proposal to update the relevant document on the observer procedures and guidelines. Note that the first FCP you'll see, when opening the PR, is the original one from 2024. There have been updates since, and it's now updated to reflect our current practices, which is to say that observers participate in meetings; at the same time, this is a business meeting, and so there are various guidelines to help people understand how the meeting works. Manish and Jakub both gave some feedback; I made some corresponding updates. I think the next step here is probably to FCP it.

TC: Does anyone have any thoughts? Jakub.

Jakub: I was curious about the historical context. Today, the council meetings are quite open. For the past year, we've had the PDs here, Lori, and Project members; people join at the last minute without a big approval process. I think it's good, but the previous policy was more locked down. What was the reason for this?

TC: The original Council was just very cautious about it, and there was a process of opening up; there were concerns about people being disruptive or taking information out of context. We've opened up, as you noted, over the last 2 years, and I think it's been going well. I don't think we have had any problems. So this is updating the document to reflect the warmth and inclusivity and opennness that has been our policy over that time. Jack.

Jack: I think I'm the only one from the original council that actually made this. I'd not say we did it because we thought it'd be disruptive. The expectation was that we had a lot to do, and if we kept it to council members, we could get things done much quicker. I think this was the primary motivation, not disruption, though I think disruption could cover that. I think it's true that having a more open policy is good. It has come up in the past that there's been a lot of discussion outside of council members. The council should be aware that more open meetings could take more time.

TC: Good. Thank you for that context. Jakub, maybe, would you like to propose FCP?

Jakub: Yes. I'll read it once more. Don't remember the final stance on using the chat. It's something I wanted to take a look at. I agree with someone who said the chat can be confusing when discussion is happening. I think there was something discouraging it, but I'll check and propose FCP.

TC: Thank you. Josh.

Josh: There is some guidance that says "don't have side conversations in the chat", and I think it's worth preserving. It's a good thing that we can adjust this policy if something comes up (like if a lot of members join and we have to limit participation again). It'd be nice to have a large audience here because that'd imply more participation. There are worse problems to have.

TC: Makes sense. Any other thoughts on this matter?

TC: Hearing none. And Jakub, the answer to your question is that the use of the chat is in a different policy that this doesn't touch; it's in the decorum policy.

### "Formulate a `trusted-contributors` marker team" leadership-council#325

- **Link:** https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/issues/325
- **Labels:** T-leadership-council, I-council-nominated

TC: This was proposed by Jieyou Xu. Go ahead Jakub.

Jakub: We discussed this in the infra team. There's now a marker team for people who want to use the dev desktops. We also have people regularly contributing to the compiler or the `rust-lang/rust` repo, and some of them want to run try builds or benchmarks while not being team members. So we're thinking we could have a team for providing access to performance benchmarks, etc. I don't love the name because it implies there are untrusted contributors, but the concept is important.

Josh: +1 to this. The only reason we limit access to try and perf is that they're resource constrained. There's no reason other than rate-limiting. We want to clarify that this is purely for access and not a membership in the Project (e.g. it doesn't fit in the Project organizational structure, or report up through a team and have a council member).

TC: Taking my hand: I think this makes a ton of sense. Mechanically, the one thing I saw in the proposal was that it says that people would be removed if inactive for three months. I'd propose extending that, maybe to a year. It's common for people to disappear for 3 months, and it'd be a lot of churn to remove and readd people for such a short interval.

TC: One question I had, for those proposing it, is to what degree this isn't a kind of `compiler-contributors` team? Many teams in the Project have `-contributors` teams to serve a similar purpose. Just want to understand the different a bit better between this and that. Rémy.

Rémy: We used to have a contributors team in the compiler. That was merged into the existing team and different responsibilities. For example, FCP was moved to a different subset. Joining the compiler team is now easy and easier than it was in the past. My understanding is that this marker team is for people who don't want to join the team or don't fit neatly into team-based work.

Rémy: My question is, can we fix the caching first between try builds and stuff? This is for perf and security reasons. The try builds have historically been closed off a bit. The people who issue the `bors` command do a spot check of what's being done. There are security questions about caching artifacts.

Jakub: Josh, it's not just about capacity, but also security. If you do a try build, you can override the main auto build artifacts, and with some effort you could override what goes into the nightly (maybe even what goes into the next stable). We want to solve that long-term.

Jakub: Regarding the duration, we have a policy that you have to be active in the last 6 months to remain a Project member. So this was reduced to three months for the marker team. Of course we are not automatically enforcing that today.

Jack: +1 to this overall. Being on the compiler team implies a sense of responsibility or representing the Project in some way. And I don't get that from this marker team.

TC: OK. Any other thoughts on this? Frank.

Frank: I don't think it makes sense to assume that those people need to be more active than a Project member, so I don't see why it should be shorter than 6 months. Maybe we should use the same number?

Jakub: We probably wouldn't enforce it anyway. Maybe we can just leave it at 6 months.
