This changes how we should be doing the budgeting. It means we should pull out of the programs the funds we don't expect to be spent during 2026. oli: Is everyone able to check their boxes? Can you do that after the meeting so we can get this moving? If you have concerns, we can chat about those here.

I want to make sure these program are in a healthy state going to next year. How we've done things in the past is the only way we could ensure that.

David: What we want to do when we're deciding the budget — and the PDs are approving the Foundation's budget — is make sure the council is getting enough to keep its programs going. The Foundation staff see these programs as being important and valuable. We see the benefit they're having to the Project. So everyone on the Foundation side is interesting in ensuring we have enough money to keep them going. And so it's important that when the budget is being made each year, we try to allocate as much as we can to these. There are several checks. If there were insufficient funds there, we as PDs could say that we want to change things so that the council's budget does have enough for its important programs to continue. Or, if in having these discussions, we realize that's not something that's reasonable to do, then there's always dipping into the Foundation's reserves to cover either the three months (or so) pay that you've been keeping aside in your budget to wind things down, we could cover it from that, or to fund parts of the budget. The point is that we anticipate having enough money to empty the budget every year. Putting it aside means you're not using it for things you could be using it for. And we're quite confident that you'll be able to have a budget every year that will let you cover the programs that are important. If that wasn't to happen, the PDs can do something about it. And there's a pot of money for important things to be covered from, if it came to it. That's what I've discussed with Abi. We're aligned on that. It means the council doesn't have to put aside 25% more (or whatever) just in case.

Eric: Let's say, for some reason, there's less funding available next year, and we have to say, "OK, we have to wind down the program management program in three or four months", the Foundation can cover that for some period of time?

David: I expect so. Nobody wants a situation where it comes to Decemeber and the council has zero dollars next year, leaving the people relying on that (e.g., our contractors), with nothing. That's obviously not what anybody wants. If it did come to it where the Foundation finances were really struggling (which is not going to happen — we're working hard to fundraise), what we would do is tap into the reserves so we can give those people a couple of months of runway that you would have saved away if you had been doing that anyway.

TC: By way of background, since not everyone here has gone through a full budget cycle, we don't tend to get clarity on the budget until November or December in the year. That's why we had needed to leave room to ensure continuity. As that'd be too little time to wind down a program. That's the structural importance of what David is saying and what the Foundation is guaranteeing here.
