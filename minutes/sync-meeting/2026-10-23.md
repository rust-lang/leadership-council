### Council meta discussion [leadership-council#337](https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/issues/337)

Jakub: I don't think I have anything new from last time. I didn't have time to look into the document yet. As with the budget, someone needs to investigate the items from the Zulip discussions and the survey, and the document that Jynn shared. And then bring specific topic to the Council. Since it's the last topic today, we can spend the next 30 minutes discussing this, but I don't have anything prepared.

Mark: I read the doc a while back, but haven't had the time to digest. One thing Jynn brought up is that (in an ideal world) we could delegate this work to a subteam. I wonder if there's room for us to delegate this to someone outside of the Council. I worry that we're all busy and it would be nice if we could get someone else to own that. I think that without that kind of involvement, we're not going to successfully make progress.

Pete: I like this idea. The Vision Doc team has conducted surveys and interviews, and done some analysis. There are plans to write up the results of that and how the data was collected and analyzed. I can't volunteer the vision doc team to do this work, but there might be a useful artifact coming from that work that could support someone in doing the work we're talking about now.

Rémy: Mark, are you suggesting that we summarize the documents and the issues? Or that we go through a process that involves interviews of people? Or do you think the documents are enough as a start?

Mark: I feel that there are concerns or feelings that something's not right in various areas, rather than "here's something we should do". This is as expected, but I'd like for us to say that we're willing to spend some time in these meetings to evaluate the shape of the problems and proposals. We can say whether we agree with the problems existing.

Mark: To Pete's point about someone stepping up, I've heard from people that they're waiting and looking for permission to step up to help. To the degree that we can give that permission and say that we'll hear people out and FCP, etc., that's how I'm thinking about it.  I'm open to other ideas.

Rémy: So, splitting up a structural decisions committee?

Mark: More I'm thinking of suggesting that people can step up to be on a group, we'll look at a draft charter or an initial proposal for it (something like David's comprehensibility work). We shouldn't make a group with vague objectives.  We need something more concrete, not just a general "rework everything" group.

Pete: Manish, did you have thoughts on this from a governance perspective? It sounds as though we'd have some group that's empowered to take in some things and triage them themselves or bring them to the LC as needed. It sounds related to what the LC should already be doing, in a way.

Manish: We could have a different group for this. It'd be one of those groups that comes to the LC with a proposal that they've talked with the LC about. We've tried doing governance working groups, and that didn't seem to actually work well. It's a lot of work, and a not of people are interested in doing it, and the people who are tend to have strong opinions, and those are not necessarily the best people to do it. I'm also hopeful that it will work this time, but I don't know what the secret sauce is.

Mark: Clar Fon [left a comment][clarfon-337] on this issue recently saying they're interested in helping and onboarding people. So there is interest. I'd like us to say something positive on this issue or on Zulip about the general shape, that we want Clar Fon or others to make an issue proposing something they'll own; we want to solicit such proposals. It feels like a low commitment to say that we'll read issues from people.  And it seems that people want this permission.

[clarfon-337]: https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/issues/337#issuecomment-5982396629

Oli: To avoid the failure mode that Manish mentioned, which is that we get someone from the community who is doing this, but the person runs out of time or priorities change, can we also set up a kind of check-in where we cancel that project if it fails? Not setting it up to fail, but looking to, e.g., hire someone outside the Project, or having some other fallback, so that we don't end up right here in 3-6 months. Making a plan.

Manish: Similar to what Oli is saying. We have 2 things that we didn't have in the past: 1) more project management work, more in the trenches and up to date with them; 2) the core team used to get easily overwhelmed and not be able to monitor stuff, but I think the Council is pretty good at monitoring stuff. These are good signs for this kind of delegation.

Mark: If we did structure this as a Project goal, it has a natural structure, as you ask for permission to do things and get support from a team (the council in this case), and it has a cadence for doing reporting.  So the concrete thing we could say is that we'd be happy to see Project goals cut for council asks.

Pete: It seems a good thing here in that we'd be exercising infrastructure in the Project that we already have.

Rémy: Could you explain why the structure matters? What's the difference between the Council spinning up a committee vs. the same person being involved in a Project goal? I'm not so worried about structure than the work getting done.

Mark: Historically, when we tried spinning up a committee, we were correctly reluctant to do so without knowing what they would be doing.  The failure mode is that we have a lot of problems, but they have a vague cross-team shape. I worry that we can't delegate a committee to "go fix everything". Project goals are structured around having a thing with enough details to get sign-off from a team, e.g., revising the RFC process in a certain direction. Then the ask is the council helping to organize the cross-team buy-in to make that official. There is a bunch of stuff in this space where people have had ideas but haven't know how to drive those. This would give them a mechanism to do those that has a lower barrier to entry than a council committee feels like it has.

Rémy: I don't think we should spin up a committee to fix everything. I think one of the reasons that governance groups had issues is that there are a lot of problems to solve, and working on these problems tends to burn people out, push them out of the Project, burn relationships, etc. This is something the council has been tasked to do.  So spinning it out feels bad to me.  Filling the cracks between teams feels like something for us.

Pete: The Project goals process might help with finding atomic sets of related issues, and allow for those interested individuals to improve parts of the Project that they care about, and can help gradually refine the intended outcome.

Mark: To Rémy's point, essentially what I'm getting at is that it may be the action requested of us to spin up committees. I don't think we'll find 3-4 people interested in this, and it feels weird to create a committee made of a single person (we kind of did that with T-comprehensibility, and that work hasn't really made progress, or at least, it's been slow, due to that). I think that goals helps provide a structure, and that structure helps in coordinating cross-team activities in a specific amount of time. I think the council ask should always be doing FCPs. But starting with that is often the wrong thing.

Manish: I agree with Rémy that this is really the work of the council and it feels wrong to push this work onto others, but I also feel that the Council must. The core team became overloaded by trying to do everything. We can have Council involvement without the Council running this. If we have a team doing this, even one person, collecting data, and the person is coming to us with feedback and asking us what we think, then it doesn't fall on our shoulders, and we can dial up and down our efforts as time permits. So I think we can be involved without it taking over our time or being our team's responsibility.

Pete: I want to separate two things: 1) whether we believe there are things that could be improved governance-wise and that could be improved with our consultation as LC;  2) whether we're okay with delegating some of these things. On (1), can we get thumbs up?
