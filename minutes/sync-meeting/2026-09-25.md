### "[launching-pad] Re-charter the Launching Pad team" leadership-council#170

- **Link:** https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/issues/170
- **Labels:** A-teams, I-council-nominated

Pete: I did a bit of reading and heard from people. It seems that the lauching pad was viewed as a temporary thing, which seems like it's no longer the case. Where do we want to take the discussion here?

Mara: There's some history that's useful to go over, and ideas for the future. Some viewed launching-pad as temporary, but I don't think that the people involved saw it that way. Rather, teams sitting under launching-pad were supposed to be there temporarily. We start a new team, dump it into the launching-pad and then figure out a proper place for it. But now we've collected a bunch of teams there. Josh, do you want to explain more of the history before I go over possible ideas for the future?

Josh: When we originally created the charter for the LC and the new project structure, the launching-pad was effectively a catching area for teams that didn't have an obvious place to go. To make sure every team had a LC representative, we needed to hook them into the tree structure. There was some ambiguity around which part was temporary, but there was always the desire for this grouping to not be permanent. There was supposed to be outbound pressure for launching-pad to find proper homes for teams, whether the homes existed or needed to be created. We had the expectation that some larger organizational changes would be needed. We didn't make those reorganizations to avoid controversy when first drafting the charter, given there was already controversy.

TC: As later history, the 2024–2025 council decided that we were happy with the launching pad continuing to exist and with there being teams that would remain in the launching pad indefinitely. We expresed this by putting new crosscutting teams there with no intention of moving those teams out. E.g., the Content team and Edition team were chartered that way. I'd be interested to hear from Mark here as well, as he was also on that council.

Mark: I don't remember all the details, but my vague recollection is that, while I don't know if permanent is the right word, there was no strong desire to recommend reorganization at the time.

Josh: The major ambiguity back then was whether it was okay to make any new team in LP, or was it only for teams that were initially stuck in it at the time of creation of the Council, and everything new should be outside of the LP. Some wanted it to be a ratchet, so the problem didn't get any worse. But ultimately, that earlier council decided it was okay to put new things into Launching Pad, without having a specific idea of where they go in the future.

Mara: It's fair to say that the LP's history was chaotic. Seems like we should talk about the present and where we can take LP today.

Mara: When this started, LP was truly a collection of random things. This has changed over time (some things moved out, disappeared, or moved in). Nearly all of the teams in LP today have something in common, vaguely described as "management and communication" (which sounds very corporate). To me it makes a lot of sense to have these teams together (security is an outlier, but with the new t-security plans that can be its own top-level team). The name "launching pad" doesn't make much sense though. If we rename it, the only question that remains is, do we feel comfortable having to find a place for new teams in the future?

Josh: I completely agree with you that over time the teams in LP started to have a common element. The launching pad has described the negative space of the rest of the organization. We need a team of meta or a team of communication policy. I do think we should create it as a new top-level team and move all these things into it. I do think we should keep launching pad as an empty catch-all team. It had value as a place for teams that don't fit into our current organizational structure.

Jack: To Josh's point, that's not entirely wrong. If we moved all teams out of LP and kept it, would it still make sense for LP to have a Council rep without any team to represent?

Josh: When I said "clean up the corner cases", that's exactly what I had in mind. I think Mara has been heavily leading a team that's this big common element of meta, and I think we should offer the opportunity to say, you want to move over and be the representative of this meta team that's the same as what you've been leading. Then we can say "launching pad needs a representative if and only if it has a team in it".

Mara: In the chat someone asked: what about embedded (and gamedev etc)? But those should just be user societies outside of the team hierarchy, as we've been planning. (Though that plan is probably standing still..)

Mara: I wanted to say what Jack said. I can see a reason for keeping LP, but if it's empty, then we could say it gets no rep, but then the moment some tiny team joins it gets its own rep -- it's a weird situation.

oli: The mod rep could represent the launching pad team / unparented teams, as people who are not otherwise represented.

Josh: I'd be hesitant to add more onto the moderation team's plate than it already has, but if they were enthusiastic about taking this responsibility for representing the unrepresented, that seems reasonable. Hopefully once we move this big cohort of teams out of launching pad, launching pad will usually have very few teams. And if launching team ever grows substantially again, it could get a dedicated representative again.

oli: It's more on the rep, and the rest of the mod team wouldn't have to do anything about that. That's kind of what we already do. Like the idea of "representing the unrepresented", that's something mods already do.

Pete: What I've seen is that it seems like the Project and teams seem to work well when they're bottom up, and there's a clear sense of cohesion. For example, I've been told that there were challenges in the past related to a subteam of devtools. There was a feeling that maybe devtools could work as a middle managmenent in a way, and sort out the things of the subteams -- but that issues percolated up to the LC. Having teams in the launching pad report directly to the LC may help to give those teams a voice that they may not otherwise have.

Mara: LP right now doesn't have any direct members. I'm not even a member. I feel like if we were to create a team about "management and communication", then that team should have members. That could be a useful position to coordinate a bunch of things that aren't (e.g. between the various teams that do public commnunication). Not to say that a team should be dictating rules, but at least we'd have a place for communication about these things. I'd be happy to volunteer as initial team lead of this team.

Fon: Concurrent themes I've noticed. 1) The only team besides LP that I might put meta work under is the Council. Currently only the LLM-policy team is directly under the Council. 2) Maybe there's too much focus on the concept of a high-level team. From WGs, it's been clear to me that each individual team doesn't fit under one of the top-level teams. Compiler, libs, lang, devtools share a bit in common with a lot of the different parts of the Project. Saying you have to pick which you're under may be leading people astray. Maybe rather than focusing on the top-level organization, we could say that you're allowed to have that, and say that the council rep is a step below that.

Josh: We've talked in devtools about whether some of our teams would fit better elsewhere (e.g., whether half of devtools might fit better in compiler). It's a good heuristic that top-level teams don't fit inside some other team. When we think about larger reorganizations, we can think about whether some of those teams should be grouped.

I do think that having a meta team makes a lot of sense because there are several other things that we currently don't have (e.g., public policy, working with governments, specific aspects of communication/writing/drafting work). Makes sense to have dedicated members for this team.

We've had a problem we've talked about where the council delegates something, and that delegation doesn't have a home, it just kinda exists adjunct to the Council. I wonder if it makes sense to put that into a meta team of some kind.

Jack: Counterpoint: if you can have people that'll be active, and there are things to do, then having a team on top of this is important. But many of those things could be said about devtools, and these teams will over time tend to not be active. Who else will be on the team and who'll put time and energy into it? We should be careful to not expect that people will do extra work indefinitely. It's also OK to lean on the way that we're already trying to do better, which is communicating across teams. We don't necessarily need a superteam of teams to do that communication.

Mara: Without a team for this we might miss out on people who want to do this type of work. Right now, much of the work I'm doing for the Project doesn't fit anywhere. If we were to have this team, it'd be a perfect match for what I'm doing. I can't immediately think of others who would be excited to jump into that team. But if we create a space, then maybe people will come.

Pete: To bring this to a conclusion, Mara, it sounds like you have an idea. Do you, or anyone here, want to try bringing something forward for what to do about this issue?

Mara: I'd like a vibe check on whether this direction I've been describing is desirable.

Mark: No concerns on moving things out of launching pad. I don't think we should shut down launching pad before we have a path forward for embedded and other WGs.

Mara: Yes. This is not something to rush. I don't think we're in a rush, but it's good to have a direction and work slowly towards that. If anyone has ideas, maybe just message me. I'm not expecting to have a plan in the next meeting, but maybe something in a few months.

Josh: I'd be happy to help you with structural drafting and chartering a team.

Mara: Sounds useful; thank you.

Pete: Perhaps by the next time or later we could revisit this if there's a concrete proposal.
