### "Review and ratify the Funding team charter" [leadership-council#336](https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/issues/336)

- **Labels:** T-leadership-council, I-council-nominated

Jakub: The Funding team was established around RustWeek and we were tasked with writing a charter. I shared the draft we came up with by the end of September. It's on the Council to take a look and provide comments. Some members already left feedback. Same question as above: how should this be approved? I think FCP should be enough.

Mark: I think FCP is enough. But it would be a good idea for Council members to bring it up with team agendas as an FYI. Lots of people care this, in particular the conflict of interest items.

Tyler: I left some comments. The most significant one is about the conflict of interest policy. I'm a bit skeptical that the policy requires only a rubber stamp from the Council that defines someone on the team. I feel there should be more of an expectation of due diligence the Council needs to do. E.g. default being it's not allowed unless the Council makes an exception.

Mara: We only put people on the Funding team that we already trust and are important to the Rust Project. It seems that these are all people we would want to fund anyway. Obviously they shouldn't prioritize themselves, but I don't worry about conflict of interests there much.

Fon: I'm with Mara on this one. We should also word it from the perspective of what the alternative would be. There are only a few options: one is that the Funding people already have funding. Or there would only be people on the Funding team who are externally employed. Historically, we've seen issues with that as the tech industry changes priorities and it also results in people who don't have a lot of experience with how funding works because they're employed. And third is that they're funding themselves. We need to be careful, but there are backstops with mods/LC getting involved. I don't think it's an issue.

Manish: I wonder if we could go the other way. Mara said these are people we'd want to fund anyway. What if we said that every member on the Funding team that's not already employed is eligible for X amount of funding. They don't get the funding automatically by being on the team. But if they propose funding for a project, they're already eligible as long as there's a project that's actually fundable.

Tyler: I like that as an idea. I do think a conflict of interest can exist even if you trust people involved. I personally witnessed a situation where a group responsible for ratifying a decision approved one of their own and they ended up making a wrong decision even though nobody did the wrong thing personally.

David: I don't mind Manish's proposal, but I don't mind how the text as proposed is written that there should be some check from the Council that says it's okay. It doesn't have to be a big check, just something.

Jakub: Regarding Manish's proposal, there are two related sources of Funding that the team members can ask for. The Funding team member could also ask for funding for work that's not the Funding team work.

Manish: Yes

Jakub: I wrote the conflict of interest rules to handle situations like mine: I'm on the Funding team, I'm also on the Council and my current funding is running out next year so I might be asking for some funds for infra/compiler next year. If we can make it work for someone in my situation, it will likely be a good generic solution.

Lori: I want to echo what Jakub said. The entire time we were going through this process, this has been top of mind. A lot of care went into this.

Fon: No matter what we do, conflicts of interests are going to be issues. Even the best-intentioned people might have them. Anything can happen. What's important is that we handle them. Make sure everyone's constantly being held accountable. That's probably the most helpful thing we could do, rather than coming up with a strict policy which results in people who need funding not getting it.

Lori: There are three other people on the Funding team. The way it works is we look at how much money we have, how much need is on that team. If that person would look like they might have a conflict of interest, we'd get to the Council and bring all the information to verify this is the right person, having mods in the loop. It's a very thought-out process that we go through. It's not just "Jakub needs funding, let's get him on the compiler".

Tyler: If it's structured that way, that makes sense to me. I'd like to see more wording like that in the policy. The other thing I wanted to say: I think we can have a lightweight process for base level funding and then a more rigorous policy for grants.

Jess: Everything people are discussing sounds reasonable. I advise we have a really explicit method for someone suggesting themself to receive funding. I've seen this in the RIL (Rust Innovation Lab) where people have a project, but don't want to overstep. Having a formulaic process for people to self-nominate would be helpful.

### "Figure out what we want from our budget for 2027" [leadership-council#339](https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/issues/339)

- **Labels:** T-leadership-council, I-council-nominated

Jakub: Last year we had meetings with the Foundation where we were discussing the budget next year. I talked to some PDs and it seems this process will repeat. We should proactively think about the sort of programs we want to continue, new things to start, things we'd like to not fund anymore. The Foundation won't just give us a number, but we have to ask for a budget. There were changes e.g. Tomáš is now hired by the Foundation, that will be reflected in the budget. These discussions will also effect how much budget may be available to the Funding team or even the Council. Let's think about the list of things we'd like to fund for the next year.

David: The goal is to make sure we're being deliberate about funding. Ask as much as possible, but it's important to be deliberate about that. That's also useful for us. We want to be able to say to the members that we're being deliberate about how money is being spent. Put in as much as you can think of and we'll do our best.

Tyler: I don't know where I'd see the current budget -- it'd be helpful to have a link. As a Project member not on the Council I have some ideas for things I'd do budget asks for. How do we register these?

David (unspoken): https://rust-lang.zulipchat.com/#narrow/channel/392734-council/topic/council.20budget.20updates/near/624604839 is the current budget

Jakub: Re: how to view the Council's budget: it's not easily visible, which we should fix. David's been keeping some charts with our budget. It would be good to put that somewhere visible online. I'll take that action item.

Jakub: Regarding asking for budget: I don't remember whether the Council asked Project members in the past on what they'd like to fund. I think it would be nice to do that e.g. on Zulip.

Mara: I feel there are certain items we want as much money as possible. For travel etc. we can predict how much we need. But for funding maintainers, we can use everything we get. Am I seeing that wrong?

Tyler: Regarding gathering ideas, I'd suggest doing it in a more structure way than just creating a topic on Zulip. We have our representatives on the Council. We can do it through that lens.

Mark: To reply to Mara, I do think it's a useful question to the Funding team of how much we could practically use. I brought up on the Funding charter to know how many people to fund and how many people we wanted to fund but didn't have money for. That might be better than a blanket ask.

Jakub: I agree we all want infinite money for the maintainers, but it's good to look at how much money the Council has put to the Funding team this year and maybe check how much money the Council will get next year and use that as a baseline. The expectation for the people we're funding is to give them the same amount next year. Even as a funding team member I'm currently unclear on the interface between the Foundation and the Project Priorities funding. Does that have to go through the Council or could we have the Foundation dedicate some budget to RFMF every year outside of the money the companies give every year? That's in flux but it would be good to discuss that between the Funding team, Foundation and Council.

David: What we're doing now is working out how this year-by-year will work. Direct funding we get from member companies for RFMF goes directly to the Funding teams' pot. Otherwise, we give a lump sum to the Council and they decide what funds go where. But that's something we should definitely work out.

Tyler: I see a couple of roles for the Council: one is to be the front door for the Project. The Foundation gives funding to the Council and they distribute it, have accountability & communication structure around that. The other is they can provide high-level guidance and strategy, priorities for the Project. That's worth discussing later.


### "All Hands 2027 hotel pre-booking" [leadership-council#340](https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/issues/340)

- **Labels:** T-leadership-council, I-council-nominated, A-budget

Mara: We were talking about budget and savings on the travel grants. The majority is used for Rust Week because All Hands. The hotels are expensive, 200-220 EUR/night. We found one hotel that's willing to give us room for 148 EUR/night if we book all the rooms at once. I want to do that, but that means if people get travel grant, we should just assign a room to them. If the Council & Foundation are okay doing that, RustNL can set that up and send the invoice next year when we have budget. We're able to take that risk and we can sell the rooms we don't get occupied to other people traveling to RustWeek.

Jakub: I love that idea. For us to make this decision, what are the pros and cons? Do you have an expectation of how much money we could save on the travel grants? We have the data on how much money people spent on accommodation last year.

Mara: I believe 47 people used travel grants, we're saving around 50 EUR per night per person. Roughly $17k USD saved.

Jakub: Would that mean we'd not provide travel grants for accommodation for RustWeek at all?

Mara: This hotel is a bit further than the other ones (we'd send a bus or something). For accessibility we could provide people the flexibility, but that should be an exception.

Jakub: If this is further or people might prefer other hotels, how do we motivate them? What happens if people make use of this and the rest book a hotel themselves?

Jack:  Could we book min 50 but then give others the option to also book rooms at the same rate?

Mara: This particular hotel has about 75 rooms, we're booking 50 so the majority of what's available.

Manish: I think it's not that hard to convince people to do the hotel block. I've never seen a conference with a hotel block that didn't get used.

Mara: It's not a reservation that people book into. We pay people in advance and assign rooms to them.

Rémy: Could we have an idea of the total cost? People were asking for travel grants for other conferences this year. How much of the Council's travel grant would be used up for this?

Mara: $50k USD if all the rooms are used.

Lori: I'd like to get a little granular, how many nights are included, how many people -- not everyone went for the full time, if there are people that couldn't go -- that's money that's now out.

Mara: RustNL would pay up-front and would send you an invoice for rooms and nights that were actually used by the Project members. So there's no cost to the Foundation and Council. What we ask is the Council recommend that people use this.

Lori: Before it's decided, I want to bring Abi in because she's handling the finances.

Fon: From the perspective of the fact that ultimately there could be other people who could take these rooms, that seems like a very reasonable thing. Even from the perspective for my having had a situation where I had to end up calling the RustConf hotel. If I don't have to deal with anything related to booking, that seems like a win/win situation.

Jakub: If we go with this, what will happen with the rest of the Travel grants? If we save $17k, will we offer less money for the travel grant? Or offer the same amount? What would be the benefit. This seems like a big risk for RustNL.

David: One small risk is: the travel grants as we've set them up today will benefit RustWeek. As the other conferences don't have All hands next to them, they get a lower travel grant proposal. That's okay because people make their own cases where they go. But this changes things because the Project is doing more of a choice of which conferences people go to. And that's less unbiased than before. It would be different if we did it just for the All Hands days, though.

Tyler: Next steps: it'd be helpful if there was a doc/issue with the financials we were discussing, who's taking on what risk. And that needs to go by Abi from the Foundation and then routed back to the Council. Does that sound right?

Mark: That wouldn't hurt. But the way I'm understanding is: we as the Project are committing to exactly one thing: tell grantees that if they want cheap rates, they can get a hotel with less friction by clicking here to get a room. We're not committing to anything else, anything that's different from previous years. RustNL will pay for the rooms, we're not committing to paying anything else.

Mara: That's correct. I agree with what David said if the Rust Project / Foundation booked the hotel, but that's not what's happening. I'm saying RustNL is planning to do this, all I'm asking as a commitment from the Project to steer people towards this hotel so we can take this (small) risk. Any other conferences could do the same.

Mara: Can we get a vibe check?

(lots of thumbs up appeared on the screen)

Tyler: No concerns from me but I'd like to see it written out.
