### "Create an LLM committee" leadership-council#308

- **Link:** https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/issues/308
- **Labels:** T-leadership-council, S-needs-decision, I-council-nominated

TC: oli, you proposed this. Tell us about it.

oli: I've been talking to people about the rust-lang/rust policy by jyn. A lot of people who either don't have veto power or checked boxes but might still have concerns they don't wish to raise, and people who want to raise concerns but don't because of reasons. A lot of that is bad. We can probably finish the policy and land it (not many checkboxes left). If it lands, it could create or continue creating discontent in the community. There are also concerns that the policy will be hard to change. This brought me to ground zero, and I went back to the opinions that Niko gathered from the community. I had an idea to create a team to create a policy without running into a deadlock. I want a situation where a small group of people discusses it and interacts with the wider community. The current discussion threads are too fast and unmanageable. I don't think the council can have this conversation in a way that leads somewhere. We've talked before about LLM stuff; we can't have a proper conversation that about this that gets us anywhere. I hope we get a good set of people for this from the start. It's more important hat we can iterate on it than get the perfect thing at the start.

TC: You mentioned that the council discussed this in the past and that we weren't able to make progress. My sense is the opposite: that the council largely hasn't discussed this. I'm curious to hear, what do you have in mind exactly regarding the past experience?

oli: Talking about the mods slop policy. Back then, we didn't have a productive discussion. It was frustrating for everyone involved. We're supposed to delegate anyway. We're supposed to make sure the right things happen but we don't have to be the one to make them happen. I can give an overview of my idea and we can discuss, or you can read the issue. I'd like us to FCP it by the end of this meeting.

TC: I appreciate you putting this together, and agree that it makes sense to have a group of people tasked with investigating this and collecting information from across the Project, in the model of what Niko had proposed. In terms of the policy ratification aspect of it, I think a cross-project policy such as this is a council concern. This would be one of the most consequential Project policies in recent memory. I'd be therefore concerned about delegating that part.

oli: We'd be concentrating the responsibility in fewer people that are in the council. We basically need a team of people that can make decisions and change the policy without deadlocking. This is not a one-way door. We can keep discussing and raising points on it. It was hard to approve the Funding team even though we all agreed it should happen.

TC: I understand and appreciate what you're saying. At the same time, the council is the delegated body for these decisions — we're directly accountable to our teams for them. This is obviously something people across the Project care deeply about. That's why we're seeing the kind of interest, and also the polarization and consternation, that you correctly cite. I feel that we're not doing our jobs as the council if we cast a vote of no confidence preemptively in our ability to do it. I don't think we've yet invested the time in this, as a council, and so it's premature to suggest that the council is unfit to do this.

David: oli makes a lot of good arguments why we need this. I don't think this has to stay a matter of the council. I don't think that has to be an indictment of the council.

Remy: I think I agree with oli and David here. The council should have driven the discussion, and we had the opportunity to do so. I'd happily FCP merge oli's proposal.

Jack: I want to explicitly point out that the council was chartered to delegate as much as possible. When it can delegate it should.

Josh: 1) Jack took the words right out of my mouth. We're responsible for the things that fall out of the cracks. This doesn't mean that we're responsible for the decisions themselves. I'd be enthusiastically in support of the proposal in most of the ways. Concern: the proposal as written preempts the current policy (https://github.com/rust-lang/rust-forge/pull/1040). And the current policy is a policy for rust-lang/rust, and it makes sense to ship the policy for r-l/r and in the meantime work on a Project-wide one. We may allow shipping the r-l/r policy and approve this committee to create a Project-wide policy. I don't see another path by which we achieve a policy.

oli: If we create this committee, these proposals will die out because of lack of checkboxes. Other solutions: as part of the committee, we can merge the policy and task the committee with editing it. The committee is explicitly established to resolve the concerns. If anyone doesn't like a part of the policy, they raise a concern, and the committee resolves that. I put in the charter the point about avoiding holdup and deadlock. The LC is responsible for this team, but the team makes the decisions. If they stop doing their job, we change the team membership.

Josh: Disregarding minor details about the charter, the approach of ratifying the current proposal and tasking the committee with editing it evaporates all my concerns. I don't want to reset the effort done so far.

TC: My view is the opposite. I wouldn't want us, in forming the committee, to simultaneously ratify a starting point for them they weren't earlier involved with. That would set up challenges for the committee.

oli: I'm not that worried about it considering they can edit it. People will be annoyed at things, but the committee should move us to a better place. We don't need a good policy right now. We just need a policy and they will make sure that it gets into a good place and they can remove change restrictions, whatever. I only added the broadest guardrails that the committee has the power to change the policy, and that we can change the team if it doesn't work. It is the job of the council to pick the right people that can have consensus, that can change things within two weeks and not get boggled up in all kinds of details because they can agree with each other. They can be okay with each other that sure this is gonna be biased for one of the very strong polarizations that we have. It doesn't matter because we're gonna make changes in another two or three or four weeks. That's fine, that's the whole point, to get ride of the blockade of everybody is afraid we'll be stuck with something that sucks.

Josh: Agree with almost everything. Nit: we need a good enough policy that people can live with. Let's not accept a bad policy. The fact that we've iterated towards a livable policy makes me agree with the spirit of what you're saying.

TC: There are governance and procedural concerns I'd have with adopting rust-forge PR 1040 in its current form as a starting point for the committee. There were many changes made to this policy at the end of the process, after people checked their boxes. There are outstanding concerns about that, and I wouldn't want to bless that state with our decision.

TC: Separately, the most worrisome part of this overall situation is how it has polarized the Project, and I'm seeing that framing here too, in the proposal, with the idea that its membership is...

oli: It's been changed. It's now just four to five people with certain conditions set on it. We want to make sure people feel comfortable contacting at least one person in the committee.

TC: OK, I'll review the update (it was changed 4 minutes ahead of the start of this call).

TC: To restate my concern about taking this out of the council's hands: creating a committee will not make the strong feelings and concerns that people have about this issue, across the Project, disappear. Those will need an outlet somewhere. Because LC members are selected by their teams — and can be removed and replaced at any time — we're the most *directly* accountable cross-team body. If we delegate the decision here, this accountability is *less* direct. Given the significance of this matter, more direct accountability seems better.

TC: And I'm not sure that we need a team changing the policy every two or three weeks, as suggested. I don't know whether that would be healthy. I suspect we'll want stability here.

Eric: Responding to Josh about a livable policy: the forge policy — I couldn't live with that. I'd rather not have one. And I would also agree with TC that having a policy that's frequently changing isn't healthy for the Project. I would prefer stability.

oli: It's possible to change it quickly, but it doesn't mean it will happen in practice. The point is that the team has the power to and not get boggled up in the discussion. They can update things, add exceptions, change the root rules for specific repositories. It's just the ability to quickly change things and improve things and not get bogged up in months-long discussions. It would be more productive if people could reach out to one of the members of the committee who understands them well with their concern and rely on them. I've added things where you can raise concerns to LC about individuals that are blocking it, that are raising it. For the no-policy-is-better, it's what mods have to live with every day, and it sucks because it's very random, and people get angry when you close things, it's a mess.

Josh: About stability, I agree with oli's point that we need something that can be responsive. AI itself changes at a rapid rate, so we can't afford to have a mechanism for change that takes 3-6 months to establish a change. I don't think that we'd have a policy that churns rapidly with this team, rather one that adapts as necessary.

TC: Two points: 1) I want to raise a separate concern about loss of team autonomy, and 2) I want to see if we can refocus on the intersection of agreement.

On one, teams are already creating their own policies. E.g., crates.io has done this. I'd have concerns about chartering a committee that could override the choices teams make about their own spaces. The situation on `rust-lang/rust` is special, and I don't think what might be needed there generalizes to other teams whose spaces are more clearly and uniquely owned.

On two, as mentioned, I agree with Oli that setting up a committee to investigate, talk with people, and draft a policy makes sense. I see the value there, and that seems the intersection of agreement. I just don't want to delegate the FCP.

Josh: 1) I think in practice, if we tried to delegate to a committee the proposal without the FCP and evolution of the policy, then we're holding sufficient control that we'll end up relitigating it at the council level rather than meaningfully delegating. 2) While I'm for team independence, we need a Project-wide policy. We don't want people to have uncertainty of which policy applies. We need a uniform way of handling it, and ways to handle the common spaces of the Project.

oli: I don't expect that committee to just randomly change team policies without talking to them. I don't expect it to be a forced thing that results in people complaining to the council. The point is to establish things people can refer to and rely on. The things can change, exceptions can be added, etc. We just need to find the right people and let them handle it. And we definitely need to delegate the FCP, otherwise this will be dead from the start.

TC: The value I see in delegation is when a team can specialize and develop special expertise (i.e., greater expertise than other teams have in that area). I don't see how this applies here, with respect to overriding the policies of other teams. I don't see why this team would know better than the crates.io team about what policies the crates.io team needs. And this is not how we handle other team-specific policies. This is my primary concern on team autonomy.

And to the degree that the suggestion here is to adopt the rust-forge PR 1040 policy as a Project-wide policy (in the interest of having something quickly) — echoing the concern Eric Huss raised a moment ago — I think that would be highly destabilizing.

oli: That policy is for r/l-r. Then they can edit it and you can figure out a global policy. It doesn't have to randomly apply a new thing for everyone. As I keep repeating myself, this will be a team of people we trust to do the work well. The best thing to do is to create a team we have control over, and let them do it. There are no real alternatives here anymore. We tried. The other approaches have failed. We need to make something now. This is getting really, really ridiculous. We need to change something. And this is my proposal.

Josh: The other aspect I'd add is, at a meta level, whether we choose to let each team to have individual policy, or say that there's value in a single policy, is a policy decision. I don't expect us to come to agreement within council, but as part of the policy, we need to decide this than consider it as a given.

TC: Regarding your point, oli, about what we expect of the people on this committee, I agree that we can find good people whom we'll trust. But I don't think they'll have superpowers: if they're representative, they'll face the same challenges in coming to consensus that the council would face and that have been faced elsewhere in the Project.

oli: The difference is, we're picking a few people who can work well together and will trust each other. They don't have to cover the bases because they can change things in the future together. Two extreme examples in these discussions. Niko and I are opposites around using LLMs, but we can totally come up with a policy that satisfies both of us. You just need to find the right people who can work together, find compromises, find solutions, and change things.

Josh: Was going to make a similar point. A similar case is Jack and I. We both had concerns on the Forge policy, discussed it between us, and tried finding something we both agreed with. This is something the committee could do, and it's infeasible to do with 15 or more people. It is absolutely something you can find people that are capable of going meta and finding the cruxes of disagreement and collaboratively finding ways past those cruxes and finding the right solution. 

TC: With that, if it's without objection, I propose we wrap up this discussion and move on to the next item — hearing no objections, let's move on.

### "Discuss long-term place for Program Management program" leadership-council#307

- **Link:** https://github.com/rust-lang/leadership-council/issues/307
- **Labels:** A-teams, I-council-nominated, A-program-management

TC: Rémy filed LC-307, and I filed the LC-309 proposal to charter the Program Management team. Rémy, would you like to start us off?

Remy: I can explain why I created the issue. The context in which PMs and ops work have changed since we started the teams. The Funding team also changes this. There seems to be a good space for such management-related people, and I think we should show that as part of the structure. Not sure if it should be a separate team versus just being part of t-funding because that's how the regular MiR would work.

TC: To address that: according to the MiR RFC, a hired MiR joins a particular team and supports the work of that teams. When hiring a MiR, the funding team works with that team to understand its needs. What I've proposed is that we charter the Program Management team (that would specialize in the PM work of the Project); then, the funding team would work with the PM team on hiring and and management just as they would in supporting any other team.

Josh: I do think we should move PMs into the MiR program. However, I don't think we should have a team that PMs are responsible to. PM is a cross-cutting concern that is responsible to almost every team in the project. This is true of other MiRs as well. In some cases, people will be appointed to one team, or across teams. The job of the structure we established within the Funding team to maintain that is to coordinate and take feedback from the teams to evaluate performance. We should establish a team to manage MiRs, and then the performance of PMs is determined by the many various teams that they serve.

TC: I have a different view. Many teams serve other teams — consider, e.g., the work of the goals or edition teams. The council delegates to other specialized teams. Program management is the same. Doing it well requires specialized skills and techniques. Having a team supports continuous improvement on these techniques. This work was already being done in the Project, and it was these methods that formed the basis for onboarding the hired PMs.

Remy: It's a bit unclear to me what the future looks like. If we have a team for PMs, you could argue for a team for people who work on the compiler because it also requires specific skills. Not sure how it scales or how it'd be different.

TC: Let me answer that directly and then we'll go to Eric Huss. It's not about this team being a management team for those hired. The funding team still handles the MiRs in the same way that they would handle that for compiler MiRs. When you have a compiler MiR, they join the compiler team and support the compiler team's work on building the compiler. The program management team is responsible for the program management work in the Project. And the people that are hired as MiRs then join the program management team. So it's the same, in that way, as it would be for a compiler MiR. Does that make sense, Remy? 

Rémy: More so than what's written, yes. 

Eric: Remy, did you have specific concerns about how the program is being managed?

Remy: Seems that PMs are managed by t-edition but do work that's mostly not t-edition. I don't see other teams participating in this, which is worrisome. This is why I want a place for all teams to participate.

TC: Niko is the lead of the goals team. This was delegated to the goals and edition team, and Niko has been a core participant in all of the activities. So does that assuade your concern at all or no?

Remy: I'm part of the goals team too. The fact that PMs recently joined t-types meetings isn't because of t-goals, is what I'm saying.

Josh: The statement earlier was effectively that the program management team would be responsible for program management. In practice, I think the program managers are responsible for program management within the program the process the project and a program management team that consists of more than just the program managers themselves would effectively be a redundant layer of management versus establishing a team for doing the performance management of maintainers in residence, which that would cover the need of let us do the performance management for all the individual maintainers in residents. And then I don't think we need an additional team whose effective charter is do that but specifically for program managers. 

TC: I want to very specifically disagree with that. There are other people that do substantial program management work within the Project, including myself, Niko, and Eric Huss — and increasingly Pete Levasseur — and Tyler Mandry, who's also on the list of the people I invited here. So I do not agree that it is only the hired PMs that do program management work in the Project. The way we onboarded the PMs was by introducing them to the systems and processes that were already being used by volunteers within the Project to do substantial program management work.

Jakub: Need to distinguish managing the PMs and the PMs. Right now, the PM team is essentially Tomas and Nurzhan, who are both paid by the Foundation, which is a special case. You said that you want teams to be autonomous, and I agree with that. Anyone who does PM should join the PM team, and then management of hired people should be done by the Funding team. Managing of the people who are actually hired by the foundation should be done by one team that right now is t-funding, but it could be in the future a different team. I don't think it makes sense for t-edition and t-goals to be managing the PMs. Don't see what's special about these teams that they should be in the role of managing the PM's specifically.

TC: To clarify the proposal here, it's that T-funding would play its role of managing MiRs, which would include the hired program managers. So the proposal here is not that the program management team would would manage the program managers. T-funding would take on that role, and then it would work with the program management team, just as it works with other teams within the Project when it's hiring MiRs that are going to join that particular team.

Jakub: I haven't read the issue, I'm reacting to what is there now. I'd like to add that the PMs don't need a lot of management. I don't expect this to be a big deal now that they're onboarded and they can also onboard other people in the future.

Josh: If it's the case that the management of PMs will happen within the Funding team... Two decisions here: 1) whether we want to establish a PM team and how to handle that and where it should be organized, 2) establishing the idea that the management of PMs who are MiR effectively (before we had a term for that) should be consolidated under MiRs. We could treat them as separate decisions.

TC: Yes. Correspondingly, we have two issues filed.

TC: That brings us to time; we'll move to the call for clarifications.
